# Visual Branding Strategy and Usability Analysis of Household Service Interface Based on Gen Z User Behavior

**Abstract.** The digital transformation of micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) in household maintenance services remains hindered by reliance on conventional media that lacks transparency and visual consistency. This study aims to design a website interface based on a visual branding strategy integrated with cognitive design principles and evaluate its usability through heuristic evaluation and concurrent think-aloud protocols. A qualitative design research method was applied through field observation, Gen Z customer journey mapping, high-fidelity prototype development in Figma, and heuristic evaluation with 10 Gen Z participants and 3 UI/UX experts. Findings were clustered using affinity mapping and prioritized via an impact-effort matrix. Results show that the application of a professional color palette, structured information hierarchy, and a three-step booking flow increased the usability score to 87.5% (mean 4.20/5.0). The integration of Norman's Three Levels of Design, Hick's Law, Miller's Law, and the Aesthetic-Usability Effect successfully reduced cognitive load, accelerated decision-making, and built credibility perception for technical services. The final prototype presents transparent price estimation, verified portfolios, and intuitive navigation aligned with the Gen Z mental model. This study demonstrates that the synergy of visual branding and cognitive HCI principles can serve as an applicable, measurable design model ready for adoption by technical service MSMEs for digital market expansion.

**Keywords:** visual branding, usability heuristic, cognitive design, user interface, MSME, generation Z

## 1 Introduction

The Fourth Industrial Revolution has shifted MSME marketing paradigms from physical media to digital platforms that demand transparency, speed, and professional aesthetics [1]. In Indonesia, the household maintenance service sector—including septic tank cleaning, AC cleaning, plumbing, and water pump repair—still heavily relies on conventional promotion methods such as banners, brochures, and stickers [2]. These models have structural limitations: narrow information space, inability to display standard operating procedures or portfolios, and lack of price transparency, thereby reducing credibility and competitiveness [3].

Generation Z consumers (born 1997–2012) prioritize online search, digital reviews, and intuitive navigation experiences, requiring design adaptations that go beyond merely transferring physical content to screens. Their expectations demand consideration of cognitive load, mental models, and emotional resonance [4]. However, current literature still shows a gap in integrating visual branding strategies with cognitive HCI design principles for the on-demand technical service sector [5].

Based on this background, the research questions are: (1) How can a visual branding strategy be realized in a website interface for household maintenance service MSMEs by considering cognitive principles and Gen Z preferences? (2) How can heuristic-based usability evaluation and behavioral observation identify and prioritize design iterations? This study aims to design and test a UI prototype that integrates consistent visual identity, information transparency, and cognitive interaction principles to enhance service credibility and conversion. The benefits include providing a verified design template for MSMEs and improving access to transparent service information for Gen Z consumers.

## 2 Theoretical Framework and Cognitive Design Approach

### 2.1 Visual Branding and Digital Credibility Perception

Visual branding encompasses the consistency of logos, color palettes, typography, layout, and visual style that form brand identity in the digital realm [6]. For technical services often associated with "dirty" or "informal" impressions, standardized aesthetics function as signals of professionalism and trustworthiness [7]. Recent studies demonstrate that MSMEs implementing coordinated visual identities on their websites experience trust score increases of up to 42% compared to those using generic designs [8].

### 2.2 Cognitive Design Principles in Technical Service Interfaces

This study integrates cognitive HCI theories to strengthen the design foundation:

**Norman's Three Levels of Design.** The visceral level employs a navy-blue and neutral palette to create an instant professional impression. The behavioral level implements a three-step booking flow with real-time feedback. The reflective level fosters post-service security and satisfaction through testimonials and technician certifications [9].

**Hick's Law.** The main navigation is limited to a maximum of five items to reduce decision time and prevent choice overload for first-time users [10].

**Miller's Law.** Service information is chunked into structured packages (Basic, Complete, Emergency) to align with working memory capacity of 7 plus or minus 2 units [11].

**Von Restorff Effect and Gestalt Principles.** The call-to-action button (CTA) is visually isolated to enhance salience, while service cards use proximity and similarity principles to be processed as a coherent catalog [12].

**Aesthetic-Usability Effect.** A clean, minimalist design increases the perception of ease of use and user tolerance for minor friction [13].

**Mental Models.** The layout follows familiar on-demand service application patterns (hero section, categories, price estimation, booking), significantly reducing the learning curve [14].

### 2.3 Evaluation Framework and Data-Driven Iteration

Evaluation adopts Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics as the primary metric, enriched with a Concurrent Think-Aloud Protocol to capture real-time behavioral friction and Post-Task Interviews to explore qualitative perceptions [15]. Findings are analyzed through Affinity Mapping for thematic clustering and prioritized using an Impact-Effort Matrix to focus design iterations on high-value improvements with measurable complexity [16].

## 3 Research Method

The study employs a qualitative design research approach with the following workflow:

**Observation and Data Collection.** Field observation of three household maintenance service MSMEs in the Greater Jakarta area, semi-structured interviews with owners and technicians, and Gen Z customer journey mapping based on task-based scenarios.

**Visual and Cognitive Strategy Analysis.** Identification of visual elements aligned with Hick's Law, Miller's Law, Gestalt principles, and mental models of on-demand service platforms.

**Prototype Development.** Creation of low-fidelity wireframes, followed by high-fidelity design in Figma, with three iteration cycles based on feedback.

**Usability Evaluation.** Ten Gen Z participants (aged 18–26, intermediate to high digital literacy) and three UI/UX experts served as heuristic evaluators. The protocol included Concurrent Think-Aloud, Post-Task Interviews, and a 5-point Likert scale assessment across all 10 heuristics.

**Data Analysis.** Qualitative data were clustered via Affinity Mapping, quantitative data were analyzed descriptively (mean heuristic scores), and iteration priorities were determined using an Impact-Effort Matrix.

The research was conducted over 12 months with a budget of IDR 10,000,000, covering Figma licenses, participant honoraria, printing, and intellectual property and publication costs.

### 4.2 Iteration Cycle and Impact-Effort Prioritization

The prototype underwent three iterations based on think-aloud findings and interviews:

- **Iteration 1.** Hidden navigation, lengthy forms, and non-transparent pricing.
- **Iteration 2.** Addition of breadcrumbs, progress indicators, and price estimation tables above the fold.
- **Iteration 3.** Finalization of branding components, CTA micro-interactions, real-time form validation, and an interactive FAQ section.

Prioritization was conducted using an Impact-Effort Matrix as shown in Table 2.

**Table 2.** Impact-Effort prioritization matrix

|---------|--------|--------|--------|

### 4.5 Discussion

The results indicate that integrating visual branding with cognitive principles significantly improves credibility perception and task efficiency. The usability score of 84.6% confirms that design aligned with the Gen Z mental model and compliant with cognitive constraints (Hick's Law, Miller's Law) can reduce bounce rates and improve task completion rates.

The application of the Aesthetic-Usability Effect proved strategic: professional aesthetics for traditionally "unappealing" technical services served as a catalyst for digital trust [13]. Initial weaknesses in error prevention were addressed through real-time validation and terminology simplification, consistent with the iteration recommendations derived from the Impact-Effort Matrix [16].

This study fills a literature gap by demonstrating that a cognitive HCI approach can be operationalized in the context of on-demand service MSMEs in Indonesia. The structured information hierarchy and transparent pricing directly address the limitations of conventional media identified in the problem formulation, while the visual branding consistency builds the professional identity necessary for digital market competition.

## 5 Conclusion and Suggestions

This study successfully designed a website interface for household maintenance service MSMEs based on a visual branding strategy integrated with cognitive HCI design principles. Evaluation results showed a usability score of 84.6%, with significant improvements in consistency, minimalist aesthetics, and information transparency. The prototype meets the intuitive navigation and professional aesthetic preferences of Gen Z users while overcoming the limitations of conventional media through structured service presentation, portfolios, and price estimation.

Suggestions for further development include: (1) longitudinal field testing with a broader demographic sample, (2) integration of real-time booking systems, WhatsApp notifications, and digital payments, and (3) packaging the UI template as an open-source asset adoptable by national MSMEs, aligned with the five-year research roadmap. The application of an "AI as assistant, human as decision-maker" framework is also recommended for advanced feature development to maintain balance between generative efficiency and real user validation.
